### Added (2026-10-31 — on-demand parity machines stay listed while their orchestrator's heartbeat is fresh)
- The runner watcher's allowlist takes an `on-demand` marker (`windows on-demand` in the `--machines-file`, `setup-runner-watch.sh --machines linux,windows:on-demand`). An on-demand machine's runners are VMs an orchestrator starts when a job is queued for its label, so while idle it has no runner online. The watcher used to drop such a machine from `PARITY_SELFHOSTED_RUNNERS`, so no job was ever routed to its label and nothing started a VM.
- An on-demand machine stays listed while it has a runner online or its heartbeat is fresh. The heartbeat is the repository variable `PARITY_HEARTBEAT_<MACHINE>` (the machine name upper-cased, `-` as `_`), which the orchestrator writes with the Unix time in seconds, as `date +%s` prints it. It is fresh for `--heartbeat-minutes` (default 15) and may be up to two minutes ahead of the watcher's clock. A missing, non-numeric, stale or far-future heartbeat is not fresh, and the machine drops out after the usual grace.
- Plain lines keep their meaning: a plain machine is never kept by a heartbeat variable, and a line with any other marker is refused, changing nothing. The watcher's token already has "Variables: read and write", which reads the heartbeats, so it needs no new permission.
